# Exercise 2: Tomato and Egg Stir-Fry

## Ingredients

* 2 tomatoes
* 3 eggs
* 2 tablespoons neutral oil (vegetable, canola, or peanut oil)
* 2 cloves garlic, minced
* 1–2 scallions, chopped (separate whites and greens)
* 1/2 teaspoon salt (adjust to taste)
* 1 teaspoon sugar (optional, balances acidity)
* 1 teaspoon sesame oil (optional, for finishing)
* 1–2 tablespoons water (to create sauce)

## Cooking Steps

1. Wash and cut tomatoes into wedges. Mince garlic and chop scallions. Beat eggs in a bowl with a pinch of salt and a little water for fluffiness.

2. **Cook the Eggs:** Heat 1–1.5 tablespoons of oil in a wok or non-stick skillet over medium-high heat. Pour in the beaten eggs and scramble gently until just set but still soft. Remove eggs and set aside.

3. **Stir-Fry Tomatoes:** Add remaining oil to the pan. Sauté garlic and the white parts of scallions for 20–30 seconds. Add tomatoes and stir-fry for 1–2 minutes until they start releasing juice. Add water to create a light sauce.

4. Return the scrambled eggs to the pan. Sprinkle salt, sugar, and optionally a splash of ketchup. Gently fold everything together and cook for 1–2 minutes until the tomatoes are softened and the sauce thickens slightly.

5. Add the green parts of the scallions. Give a final gentle stir, and it's ready to be served!

**Cooking Tip:** Do not overcook the eggs because they can become dry. Keep them soft and gently fold them into the tomatoes.

# Exercise 3: Academic Integrity Policy

## Purpose

**To maintain a high standard of academic integrity across the Seneca community and provide the foundation for research, teaching, learning and working practices.**

## Scope

**This policy applies to current students and employees.**

## Key Definitions

### Academic Integrity

> **Within an academic environment, academic integrity is “a commitment, even in the face of adversity, to six fundamental values: honesty, trust, fairness, respect, responsibility and courage.”**

**These values help academic communities translate ideals into action and ensure that the credentials granted to students accurately reflect their demonstrated knowledge and abilities.**

### Cheating

**Cheating means obtaining or attempting to obtain credit or improve performance through dishonest or deceptive means.**

**Examples include:**

* **Self-plagiarism**
* **Impersonation**
* **Falsification**
* **Taking individual credit for group work**
* **Unauthorized collaboration or aids**
* **Undeclared AI use**

### Seneca Business Day

**A Seneca business day is Monday to Friday, excluding statutory holidays and days when Seneca has publicly acknowledged that it is closed.**

### Contract Cheating

**Contract cheating occurs when a student submits academic work created by a third party as their own for academic credit.**

### Falsification/Fabrication

**Falsification/fabrication involves misrepresenting academic work, withholding or forging documentation, or creating, altering, or reporting false or inaccurate information to gain an academic advantage.**

### Impersonation

**Impersonation means taking a test, examination, or other assessment on behalf of another individual with their knowledge and consent.**

### Official Transcript

**An official transcript is a detailed record of a student's academic history that carries an authorized signature.**

### Plagiarism

**Plagiarism means using another individual's, group's, or organization's work and presenting it as one's own without properly citing the source.**

### Self-Plagiarism

**Self-plagiarism means the unauthorized reuse of one's work from previous assignments.**

### Student Record

**A student record is a documented history of a student's educational progress found in the Student Centre.**

### Unauthorized Aid

**Unauthorized aid means using non-approved supports to complete coursework or assessments, including:**

* **Peer collaboration**
* **Artificial intelligence technology**
* **The internet**
* **Smartphones**
* **Other non-approved resources**

### Undeclared AI Use

**Undeclared AI use means using artificial intelligence to generate part or all of an assessment without a disclosure statement explaining how and to what extent the technology was used.**

### Seneca's Academic Integrity Program

**Seneca's Academic Integrity Program is a holistic and integrated model based on teaching and learning that requires participation from students and various academic and service areas.**

### Academic Integrity Violation

**An academic integrity violation is an attempt to gain an unfair advantage in any formal assessment, such as coursework or exams.**

### Hybrid Delivery

**Hybrid delivery means that some parts of a program or course are delivered online while other parts are delivered in person.**

### Flexible Course Delivery

**Flexible course delivery means that a professor teaches students in a classroom or lab while simultaneously broadcasting the class to students learning online.**

# Policy

## 1. General

* **Seneca upholds a learning community that values academic integrity, honesty, fairness, trust, respect, responsibility, and courage.**
* **Seneca is committed to delivering high-quality education and teaching excellence while supporting a positive, equitable, and inclusive learning environment.**

## 2. Academic Integrity Program

* **Academic integrity is fundamental to ensuring that academic credentials granted by Seneca accurately demonstrate students' knowledge and learning.**
* **The Academic Integrity Program requires policies, procedures, faculty development and training, student training sessions, and student life programming to be aligned with Seneca's institutional approaches to academic integrity.**

## 3. Responsibilities

* **Seneca is responsible for providing students with information and resources about academic integrity, its importance, how to avoid violations, and how to receive support.**
* **Students are responsible for informing faculty about circumstances affecting their academic performance as soon as possible.**
* **Students who witness or suspect an academic integrity violation can report it using the procedures in Appendix A.**

## 4. Sanctions

* **Academic integrity sanctions are applied according to the severity of the violation.**
* **Before a final decision or sanction is made, the student's record should be reviewed for aggravating or mitigating factors.**
* **If a suspected academic integrity violation is also connected to a violation of the Student Code of Conduct or another non-academic Seneca policy, the matter may be handled through the applicable Student Code of Conduct process.**
